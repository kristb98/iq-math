🔵 Pi & Circles: From Radius to Waves

Circles are everywhere — wheels, pizzas, clocks, planets! In this lesson you will learn the radius, the diameter, the circumference, the famous number π (pi), and the amazing Fourier technique that turns spinning circles into waves.

🧗 The Radius

The radius is the distance from the center of a circle to its edge.

Think of it as a rope tied to a post: the rope's length is the radius!

Radius = center ➡️ edge
A pizza with radius 10 cm is 10 cm from center to crust

📏 The Diameter

The diameter is the distance across a circle, passing through the center.

It is exactly two radii stuck together!

Diameter = radius × 2
d = 2 × r

🥁 The Circumference

The circumference is the distance all the way around a circle — its perimeter.

Imagine walking around a circular track: the distance you walk is the circumference.

Circumference = π × diameter
C = π × d
or C = 2 × π × r

🥧 What is Pi (π)?

Pi is a special number that connects every circle in the universe: if you divide any circle's circumference by its diameter, you always get the same number — π ≈ 3.14.

The Magic of Pi

π ≈ 3.14159…

It never ends and never repeats! People have memorized thousands of its digits.

🌊 The Fourier Technique

The Fourier technique is one of the most amazing ideas in math: any wavy shape can be built by adding simple spinning circles!

Named after the mathematician Joseph Fourier, it lets scientists break down music, voices, heartbeats, and even pictures into simple waves.

  • One circle spinning = a simple smooth wave
  • Two or more circles spinning at different speeds = wiggly waves
  • Add enough circles = ANY wave you can imagine!

Where is Fourier used?

🎵 Music apps — to show sound waves

🖼️ Image compression — like JPEG photos

❤️ Heart monitors — to find heartbeats in signals

📱 Wi-Fi and phones — to send signals cleanly
