Finding Numbers: Solving for x

Learn how to find the value of x in equations like x + 5 = 7 — and level up to squares and cubes like x² = 25.

1. The Big Idea: Keep It Balanced core concept

An equation is like a balance scale. Both sides are equal. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other — that keeps it balanced and keeps x findable.

x + 5 = 7
↓ subtract 5 from BOTH sides ↓
x = 2   ✓
x + 5 = 7 x + 5 − 5 = 7 − 5 ← do the same to both sides x = 2  check: 2 + 5 = 7 ✓
💡 Goal: get x alone on one side. Undo whatever is stuck to it by doing the opposite operation: addition is undone by subtraction, subtraction by addition.

2. Equations with + and − examples

x − 3 = 4 x − 3 + 3 = 4 + 3 ← add 3 to both sides x = 7  check: 7 − 3 = 4 ✓
x + 12 = 20 x + 12 − 12 = 20 − 12 ← subtract 12 from both sides x = 8  check: 8 + 12 = 20 ✓
x − 15 = 5 x − 15 + 15 = 5 + 15 ← add 15 to both sides x = 20  check: 20 − 15 = 5 ✓
⚠️ Common mistake: only fixing one side. If you write x = 7 − 5 for the first example, you get the right answer here by luck — but the habit breaks harder problems later. Always change both sides.

3. Level Up: x² and x³ powers

When x is squared or cubed, undo it with the opposite power move: square root for x², cube root for x³.

x² = 25 x = √25 = 5 ← what times itself gives 25? Check: 5 × 5 = 25 ✓
x³ = 8 x = ∛8 = 2 ← what times itself twice gives 8? Check: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 ✓
x² − 6 = 10  two steps: undo −6 first, then the square x² = 16 ← add 6 to both sides x = 4  check: 16 − 6 = 10 ✓
⚠️ Note: x² = 25 has two answers: x = 5 or x = −5, because (−5) × (−5) = 25 too. Cubes don't: (−2)³ = −8, not +8.
